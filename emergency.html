When to call

Call immediately if you see

Do not wait to see if it gets better.

Heart and breathing

  • Chest pain or pressure
  • Severe trouble breathing
  • Blue or grey lips

Stroke signs

  • Face drooping on one side
  • Weak arm or leg
  • Slurred speech, sudden confusion

Bleeding and injury

  • Bleeding that will not stop after 10 minutes of pressure
  • Head injury with vomiting or confusion
  • Suspected neck or back injury

Other emergencies

  • Unconscious or fitting for over 5 minutes
  • Severe allergic reaction with swelling
  • Poisoning or overdose

First aid

While you wait for help

Simple steps that can help before the ambulance arrives.

Not breathing (adult)

  1. Call for help and shout for a defibrillator
  2. Push hard and fast in the centre of the chest
  3. About 100-120 pushes a minute, until help arrives

Heavy bleeding

  1. Press firmly with a clean cloth
  2. Do not remove it. Add more cloth on top
  3. Keep the injured part raised if you can

Burns

  1. Cool under running water for 20 minutes
  2. Do not use ice, butter or toothpaste
  3. Cover loosely with a clean cloth

Choking (adult)

  1. Encourage them to cough
  2. Give up to 5 firm back blows
  3. Then 5 abdominal thrusts, and repeat

A first-aid course is the best preparation. These steps do not replace one.
